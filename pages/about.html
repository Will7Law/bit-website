Mandate

Competency-Based Vocational Training for Employment

The mission of the Board of Industrial Training is to coordinate, regulate and certify training at the artisan and/or craft level that responds to technical and manpower needs of society by ensuring that occupational competencies are attained.

Under the Industrial Training Act, Chapter 39:04 of the Laws of Guyana (Act 6 of 1910, as amended), BIT serves as Guyana's primary institution for trades training and industrial skills certification, operating under the Ministry of Labour & Manpower Planning.

Our History

116 Years of Service to Guyana

From our founding in 1910 to the present day, BIT has continuously evolved to meet the changing needs of Guyana's workforce.

1910

Foundation

The Industrial Training Act 39:04 (Act 6 of 1910) is enacted on 28 May 1910, establishing the Board of Industrial Training as a body corporate — one of the first formal vocational training bodies in the Caribbean region.

1966

Post-Independence Expansion

Following Guyana's independence, BIT expands its regional footprint to serve all 10 administrative regions, taking training to communities across the country.

1985

Full-Time Secretariat Established

In January 1985 a full-time Secretariat is established to discharge BIT's day-to-day responsibilities, headed by a Chief Executive Officer / Executive Secretary — transitioning from a purely volunteer Board model to a professional standing institution.

1990s

Programme Diversification

BIT broadens its offerings beyond traditional trades to include Information Technology, Cosmetology, Garment Construction, and Culinary Arts.

2010s

International Partnerships

Strategic partnerships with the ILO, and the Caribbean TVET Council strengthen curriculum standards and instructor development.

2025

Modern Era

BIT receives GYD $677.5 million in national budget allocation. The new $55.4M Region 6 facility is commissioned, and 15,000+ graduates have been certified with a 77% employment rate.

2026

Digital Transformation

Launch of BIT's new public website and citizen portal, featuring online applications, certificate verification, and comprehensive digital access to all programme information.

Governance

BIT's Government Structure

BIT is governed under the Industrial Training Act, Chapter 39:04. A statutory Board of ten members sets policy. A full-time Secretariat, headed by a CEO, runs day-to-day operations.

Tier 1 · Portfolio Ministry

Minister of Labour & Manpower Planning

Provides Cabinet-level direction and sets national policy on industrial training, employment, and human-capital development.

Tier 2 · Statutory Board

Board of Directors

A body corporate of ten members that meets monthly on policy and regulatory matters. No business is transacted unless at least five members are present (quorum). The Board licenses Masters, fixes apprenticeship terms, certifies artisans, and may make regulations under Chapter 39:04.

Tier 3 · Operations

Secretariat — Chief Executive Officer / Executive Secretary

Established as a full-time office in January 1985 to discharge the Board's responsibilities. The Secretariat is headed by the CEO/Executive Secretary, who oversees day-to-day operations, programme delivery, finance, and stakeholder engagement.

Tier 4 · Functional Units

Departments & Regional Units

Training & Curriculum, Finance & Administration, Human Resources, IT Infrastructure, Regional Outreach, and the apprenticeship registry — coordinated across all ten administrative regions.

Statutory Mandate

Duties of the Board

The Act prescribes nine duties for the Board. They are reproduced here in plain language.

1

License and keep a register of Masters whom the Board considers suitable to receive apprentices.

2

Issue certificates of competency to artisans who are, in its opinion, qualified to be engaged in any trade or craft to which the Act applies, and keep a register of those artisans.

3

Fix the period of Apprenticeship for the different trades to which the Act applies.

4

Procure the training of apprentices by competent Masters, and keep a register of apprentices for every trade to which the Act applies.

5

Cause apprentices to be examined and, if satisfied with their skill, issue or endorse certificates of competency on completion of their term of service.

6

Cancel licences and certificates issued under the Act when, in its opinion, it is necessary to do so.

7

Settle disputes arising between Masters and Apprentices, and transfer apprentices when desirable.

8

Arrange and direct the technical education of apprentices.

9

Appoint and pay the instructors and examiners whom the Board considers necessary for the discharge of its duties.

Legal Mandate

Our Legislative Authority

Industrial Training Act, Chapter 39:04 of the Laws of Guyana
Originally enacted as Act 6 of 1910 and subsequently amended (notably by Act 40 of 1956, Act 4 of 1972, and Act 29 of 1992), the Act establishes the Board of Industrial Training as a body corporate and sets out its duties — including the licensing of Masters, the certification of artisans, the fixing of apprenticeship periods, and the technical education of apprentices. It is administered by the Ministry of Labour & Manpower Planning.

Under this legislative mandate, BIT is empowered to:

  • Establish and operate training centres across all administrative regions
  • Develop and certify vocational training curricula aligned with national and regional standards
  • Issue nationally recognised certificates and credentials to qualifying graduates
  • Partner with industry, international organisations, and government agencies for training delivery
  • Conduct assessments and evaluations of industrial training needs across Guyana
  • Facilitate apprenticeship placements and employment connections for graduates

Statutory Framework

Four operating principles set out in the Industrial Training Act, Chapter 39:04, define how the Board is constituted and how it transacts business.

Incorporation of Board

The Board is a body corporate by the name of The Board of Industrial Training, with perpetual succession and capacity to sue and be sued in its corporate name.

Quorum

No business shall be transacted at any meeting of the Board unless at least five of its ten members are present.

Regulations

The Board may from time to time make any regulations necessary for the proper discharge of its duties under the Act, Chapter 39:04.

Application of Funds

Funds of the Board are used to discharge all liabilities incurred in performing its duties — including the award of prizes and the remuneration of servants of the Board.
