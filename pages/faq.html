Eligibility & Admissions

Who is eligible to apply for BIT programmes?
Any Guyanese citizen aged 16 years or older with basic literacy skills is eligible to apply. There are no formal academic prerequisites for most programmes, though some (such as IT and Electrical) may require basic numeracy. BIT welcomes school leavers, career changers, and working adults.
Is there an age limit for BIT programmes?
There is no upper age limit. BIT serves working-age citizens of all ages. The minimum age requirement is 16 years at the time of enrolment.
Do I need CXC or CSEC passes to apply?
No. CXC/CSEC passes are not required for admission to BIT programmes. Basic literacy and numeracy are sufficient. However, applicants with prior academic qualifications may be given consideration for advanced modules where applicable.
Can non-Guyanese citizens apply?
BIT primarily serves Guyanese citizens and permanent residents. Non-citizens may enquire about special arrangements through the admissions office. CARICOM nationals may be eligible under certain regional agreements.

Programmes & Training

How long are BIT programmes?
Programme duration varies between 6 and 12 months depending on the discipline. Trades programmes (Electrical, Welding, Plumbing, Carpentry, Masonry, Motor Vehicle, AC/Refrigeration, Agriculture) are typically 12 months. Service-oriented programmes (IT, Cosmetology, Garment Construction, Culinary Arts) are typically 9 months.
When are the intake periods?
BIT operates two main intake periods: January and July of each year. Some programmes (such as IT) may have an additional September intake. Check the intake calendar or contact admissions for current dates.
Is the training mostly practical or theoretical?
BIT programmes emphasise hands-on practical training, with approximately 80% of instruction taking place in workshops using industry-standard tools and equipment. The remaining 20% covers theoretical foundations, safety standards, and technical knowledge.
What programmes are available in my region?
Programme availability varies by region. The Georgetown headquarters (Region 4) offers all 12 programmes. Other regional centres offer selected programmes based on local demand. Visit our Regional Centres page for details on what is available near you.

Fees & Financial

How much does BIT training cost?
BIT programmes are government-subsidised, making them significantly more affordable than private training providers. Fees vary by programme. Contact the admissions office or visit your nearest BIT centre for current fee schedules. Some programmes may qualify for government bursaries or scholarships.
Are there scholarships or financial aid available?
BIT occasionally offers subsidised places and bursaries through partnerships with government agencies and development organisations. Contact the admissions office to enquire about current financial assistance options.
Are tools and materials included in the fee?
Workshop tools, equipment, and primary training materials are provided by BIT. Some programmes may require trainees to purchase specific personal items such as safety gear (PPE), uniforms, or basic tool kits. Details are provided at enrolment.

Certification & Employment

Is the BIT certificate nationally recognised?
Yes. BIT certificates are nationally recognised by employers across Guyana and are aligned with the Caribbean TVET Council framework. This means BIT credentials have regional recognition across CARICOM member states.
What is the employment rate for BIT graduates?
BIT maintains a 77% employment rate among certified graduates. Many graduates secure employment within 3 months of completing their training, with strong demand in construction, oil and gas support services, IT, and hospitality sectors.
How can an employer verify a BIT certificate?
Employers can verify any BIT certificate online through our Certificate Verification Portal. Simply enter the unique certificate code found on the bottom-right corner of the certificate to confirm its authenticity.
Does BIT help with job placement?
BIT facilitates employment connections through partnerships with organisations such as GNIC, GuySuCo, BOSAI Minerals, and DHB Corporation. Apprenticeship co-placements and industry referrals are available for qualifying graduates.

Application Process

How do I apply for a BIT programme?
You can apply online through our or submit a paper application at your nearest BIT training centre. Online applicants receive an instant reference number and confirmation email. Paper forms are available for download from our Resources page.
What documents do I need to apply?
Required documents typically include: a valid national ID card, two passport-sized photographs, and any educational certificates you hold. Some programmes may require additional documentation. A complete checklist is available on our Resources page.
How long does the application review take?
Applications are typically reviewed within 5-10 business days. Applicants may be contacted for an interview or to submit additional documents. Successful applicants receive a formal acceptance letter with enrolment instructions.
